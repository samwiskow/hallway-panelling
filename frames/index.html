Linear MDF and mould blanks allow 30 mm extra per piece and 10 mm stock-end trimming. The 2400 mm cap schedule allows 10 mm extra per piece and only 5 mm stock-end trimming. Check stock lengths first and use the spare if needed. Corner projections and actual wall angles set the final end cuts.

Fit it to the room

  1. Select a physical moulding sample before cutting. One shaped dado replaces the small profile layers shown in the render.
  2. Hallway frames run from 185 to 890 mm above the floor. Entrance frames run from 110 to 915 mm. The dado starts at 950 and 975 mm respectively.
  3. E1 uses a flat 9 mm dado. Its 11 mm frame mould leaves only 4 mm to the door before adhesive. Dry-fit the finished build-up through the full door travel.
  4. Frame cuts are measured from outside long point to outside long point. Do not subtract the moulding width. Both ends are mitred.
  5. One full frame continues behind each radiator. Check pipes, valves and brackets, and leave local gaps where needed. Radiator removal may need trade support.
  6. Left edges are measured from the clockwise start of the whole numbered wall. Set equal frame widths on site.

Still to check on site

Measure the hallway radiator floor gaps, all valve and pipe positions, the depth at the top of the skirting, and the barn-door stop position. Pipes run above the skirting: no skirting notches are included. The hallway survey retains a 20 × 10 mm closure difference. The entrance has a 5 mm width and depth difference. Check corner angles and final wall lengths.
