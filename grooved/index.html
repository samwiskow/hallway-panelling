Each sheet supplies two rows at 840 mm rough height. Finish to 835 mm. Width blanks allow 5 mm extra, plus saw loss and 5 mm stock-edge allowance per row. The PDF includes the five-sheet nesting diagram.

Linear MDF and mould blanks allow 30 mm extra per piece and 10 mm stock-end trimming. The 2400 mm cap schedule allows 10 mm extra per piece and only 5 mm stock-end trimming. Check stock lengths first and use the spare if needed. Corner projections and actual wall angles set the final end cuts.

Fit it to the room

  1. Panels run from 95 to 930 mm above the floor. The flat cap runs from 930 to 985 mm, and the lip from 985 to 1000 mm.
  2. You can replace both cap parts with one suitable 70 mm-high cap for a 6 mm panel. This needs one set of six lengths. Confirm the overlap before cutting the panels.
  3. Order grooves parallel to the 2440 mm sheet direction. Do not rotate offcuts by 90 degrees. Restart the groove layout at door openings.
  4. Confirm the usable sheet width with the supplier. The plan needs 1220 mm stock. A 1200 mm panel cannot come from a 1200 mm sheet with trimming allowance.
  5. Template pipe, bracket and socket cutouts on site. Keep valve and wall-box access clear. Use suitable trade support if radiators or electrical faceplates need removal.
  6. Five sheets fit the planned layout. A sixth is optional if groove alignment or a failed cutout needs extra material.

Still to check on site

Measure both radiator floor gaps, the full valve and pipe positions, and the depth at the top of the skirting. Pipes run above the skirting: no skirting notches are included. The survey retains a small 20 × 10 mm closure difference, so check corner angles and final wall lengths.
